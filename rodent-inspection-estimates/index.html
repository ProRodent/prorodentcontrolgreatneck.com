What the inspection actually covers.

An inspection is not a quick look around. A technician checks the attic, basement or crawl space, the foundation line on every side of the house, the garage, and any point where a utility line enters the structure. On Great Neck's smaller village lots, that usually takes less time than a larger suburban property, but the same checklist applies regardless of house size.

Older homes often show settled foundation gaps and aging roof vents. Homes built or renovated more recently tend to have fewer, more specific entry points, usually a single utility penetration or a garage door seal that was never properly fitted.

The inspection also distinguishes mouse activity from rat activity, since the droppings, gnaw marks and travel patterns differ and that distinction changes the entire plan that follows.

How the estimate works.

The price quoted is based on what the inspection actually finds, not a flat number given over the phone before anyone has seen the property. If the job is a simple trapping round, that is reflected in the price. If it also needs exclusion work or attic cleanup, those are priced as their own line items so the total is clear before work begins.

  • Every entry point found gets documented, not just the most obvious one.
  • The plan and the price are explained together, before any work starts.
  • Damage from chewed insulation or wiring is priced separately, not folded into a larger total.
The price follows the inspection. It never comes before it.

What happens after the estimate.

If you agree to the plan, work can often start the same visit or be scheduled for a follow-up appointment, whichever the household prefers. If you want to think it over, there is no pressure to commit on the spot.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.
