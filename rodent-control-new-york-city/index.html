One consolidated city, described honestly.

New York City is one consolidated city-county government spanning five boroughs. Within about 15 miles of Great Neck, that means parts of Queens, Brooklyn (Kings County), the Bronx and Manhattan (New York County); Staten Island sits outside our confirmed range. Rather than inventing a separate page for every neighborhood name inside those boroughs, which are not separate municipalities, we cover the 104 in-range ZIP codes on this one honest page.

We cover New York City from our Great Neck base, with the same inspect, trap and seal order as everywhere else. For rodents on public property or in a neighboring building, the city's 311 service takes complaints; activity inside or around your own home or business is what our visit covers.

The typical New York City call, in short.

Most calls from this area fall into one of a few categories. A phone description rarely settles mouse versus rat on its own, which is why an in-person check always comes first.

  • Activity noticed after an extended stretch without checking a seasonal or rarely used space.
  • A musty odor near an interior wall with no obvious source.
  • Droppings found in a kitchen drawer or pantry shelf.
  • A pet showing unusual interest in a specific wall, cabinet or corner.
The jurisdiction on the map does not change what the inspection actually covers.

The treatment and sealing process in this area.

Once the inspection confirms what is active, the order is always the same: garage and basement rodent control first to clear what is inside, then commercial rodent control so the same gap does not let in a replacement.

We keep damage repair, under rodent trapping and removal, separate from removal and sealing, so the pricing stays honest and specific to what was actually found.

A technician walks the exterior as carefully as the interior, since a surprising number of real entry points get missed by a walk-through that only checks indoors. That outside-in habit holds regardless of which town the call comes from.

We do not treat a less frequently called town differently from a more frequently called one. The checklist, the explanation and the follow-up standard all stay constant regardless of call volume from a particular area.
