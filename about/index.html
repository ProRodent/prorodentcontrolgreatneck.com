What we do.

We handle mouse and Norway rat problems for homes and businesses in Great Neck and the nearby towns listed on our areas page. Every visit follows the same order: inspect the property to find where activity is actually coming from, treat with trapping or exclusion matched to what was found, and seal the entry points so the problem does not simply return.

That order matters more than any single product or technique. A trap set without knowing the travel route rarely works, and a seal job done without finding every gap just leaves another one open.

How we explain a job.

You hear the plan and the price before any work starts, based on what the inspection actually found, not a generic estimate given over the phone. If damage from chewed wiring, insulation or ducts is found, it gets documented and priced as its own line item rather than folded quietly into a larger total.

Availability.

The line is answered 24 hours a day, and same-day visits are offered where the schedule allows. Whether the call comes in about a scratching sound at midnight or a routine inspection scheduled a week out, the same inspect, treat, seal approach applies.

Who we actually cover.

Great Neck itself is a small, 1.35-square-mile incorporated village on Long Island's North Shore, which means a technician based here can reach a genuinely large nearby area within a reasonable drive. That area turned out to be unusually large once we checked our coverage data and independent ZIP-code distance against it. The result was 42 individual Nassau and Westchester County towns plus the New York City boroughs within about 15 miles, which is why the areas page lists each town's ZIP code and distance so you can check your own address quickly.

We did not want to inflate that list with places that are not actually distinct municipalities. A number of neighborhood names inside Queens, Brooklyn, the Bronx and Manhattan get mentioned by homeowners the way a town name would be, but those are not separate cities or villages, they are names within the single consolidated New York City government. Our areas page treats that honestly, with one consolidated New York City page summarizing the real ZIP coverage by borough rather than a page for every neighborhood name we could find.

Why the inspection comes first, every time.

A lot of rodent control marketing leads with the treatment, the trap, the bait, the spray, and treats the inspection as an afterthought. We do the opposite because the inspection is what actually determines whether a job is simple or complicated, and skipping it is how a company ends up setting traps in the wrong spot or sealing the wrong gap.

This matters more in a place like Great Neck than it might elsewhere. A village first settled in 1644 and incorporated in 1922 is likely to have houses from many different eras, and construction age drives where the gaps are: settled masonry foundations and aging vents on older houses, a single missed utility penetration on newer ones. An inspection that treats every house the same way misses that variation; one that actually walks the property does not.

What happens when damage is found.

Rodent activity sometimes leaves behind more than droppings and a gap to seal. Chewed insulation, wiring or ductwork shows up often enough that we treat it as its own documented category of work, under rodent damage repair, rather than a surprise line added to the bill after the fact. Significant electrical damage gets referred to a licensed electrician rather than patched over casually, since that is not pest control work and should not be treated as if it were.
